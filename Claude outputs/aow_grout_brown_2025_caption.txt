"Assessment and Treatment of Acquired Stuttering: A Single Subject Study"
Grout-Brown & Theys, 2025 — Journal of Fluency Disorders

Sudden-onset stuttering in an adult with no childhood history? This single-subject case study is worth a look. An adult presenting with nine months of acquired functional stuttering went through comprehensive baseline assessment, structured treatment, and outcome tracking — with stuttered syllable frequency dropping across every speech task and the client returning to work.

The breakdown:
🔹 Assessment-first — A comprehensive baseline distinguished functional/acquired stuttering from neurogenic and developmental causes before treatment began.
🔹 Structured, hierarchy-based treatment — Progressed from baseline speech tasks through generalization, 1-2x/week, 45-60 min sessions.
🔹 Outcome — Stuttered syllables reduced across all tasks; fluency restored, client returned to work.

Clinical takeaway: When an adult presents with new-onset stuttering and no developmental history, rule out neurogenic/organic causes concurrently, then consider a structured, assessment-driven behavioral hierarchy — tracking stuttered syllables across tasks as your outcome measure.

⚠️ Evidence note: single-subject case study (evidence level 3) — a promising clinical picture, but n=1 findings shouldn't be generalized without caution.
